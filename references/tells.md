# Tells (audit step only, never load while drafting)

Per-10k-word rates, AI vs human, from Pangram (vendor data, English web). No single tell proves anything. Clusters do.

## 1. Formatting (12x)
- Markdown headers, bold, or bullets in places where people write plain prose (captions, emails, DMs, scripts).
- Bold inline lead-ins on every bullet ("**Speed:** ...").
- Emoji used as bullets, especially ✅ ⚠️ 🚀 💡 👉.
- Unusual Unicode: curly quotes in code, arrows (→), fancy separators.

## 2. Stock vocabulary (12x)
delve, tapestry, testament, realm, landscape, navigate, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, foster, harness, leverage (verb), seamless, robust, pivotal, crucial, vibrant, intricate, multifaceted, ever-evolving, game-changer, journey (non-literal), "in today's fast-paced world", "it's important to note", "it's worth noting", "let's dive in", "at the end of the day", "plays a crucial role", "a rich history of".

## 3. Punctuation rhythm (10x)
- Em dashes as the default joint between clauses. Use one at most per piece, ideally none.
- Colon reveals: "The result? ..." / "Here's the thing: ...".

## 4. Lists and triads (4-9x)
- Bullet lists where two sentences would do.
- Ideas grouped in threes by reflex ("fast, simple, and powerful").
- Parallel sentence frames repeated three times.

## 5. Contrast frames (3x)
- "Not just X, but Y." / "It's not X, it's Y." / "X isn't about Y. It's about Z."
- "Whether you're X or Y, ..."
- "From X to Y" false ranges.

## 6. Openers and closers (2x)
- Openers that restate the request, praise the question, or announce what's coming.
- Closers that summarize ("In conclusion", "Ultimately", "In short"), ask an engagement question by default, or offer more help.

## 7. Content-level slop (not in Pangram's list; the reason experts catch AI text)
- Claims with no number, name, place, or date anywhere in the paragraph.
- Balanced "on one hand / on the other" with no verdict.
- Hedge stacks: "can potentially help", "may often".
- Every paragraph the same length.
- Advice that would be true for anyone, anywhere.
- Fake specificity: invented stats, "studies show" with no study.
